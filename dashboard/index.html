How the numbers are fitted — and how far they miss

The model is a regression, not a multiple. It is fitted on the 119 live-token protocols in this snapshot (Huber IRLS, c = 1.345, 25 rounds; slope fixed at 0.45; sector offsets are shrunk medians of the live residuals, k = 20, capped at ±0.3; the growth, maturity and chain multipliers are half-damped). Every term below is measured on this entry, by the scheme named next to it:

  • Leave-one-out RMSE 0.9001 — refit 119 times, one live protocol held out each time (D1 scheme).
  • Repeated 5-fold 0.8996 (max over seeds 0, 1, 2; split by shuffled position mod 5) — the other two seeds give 0.8980 and 0.8977 (D2 scheme).
  • Walk-forward on the 35 newest launches: median miss ×2.03 — the model is fitted on launches before each one and scored on the next (D3 scheme).
  • Category-blind ×3.33 — the sector term dropped and the model refit, so a protocol's own category never helps it (D4 scheme).
  • Ranges honest: 50% of launches land in the central 50% band (stated 50–60%) and 81% in the 10–90% band (stated 70–80%).

The model is wrong by about ×3.3 on a typical launch. The board ranks by the fitted value anyway, because the same error applies to every row: the ranking is the model's best guess, not a promise.

What is not here

  • No backtest table on this page. The walk-forward test is one number above (×2.03 on the 35 newest launches); a table of past wins would flatter the model and tell you nothing about the next launch.
  • No ladder. A ladder is only printed when the shipped model beats every simpler step on both leave-one-out and walk-forward. It does not here, so no ladder is printed.
  • No T-bill in the net. The T-bill memo in each detail view is the deposit's opportunity cost at 4% a year over the hold window — a memo, never part of the net.
  • No odds in the value. Token odds are a display-only probability; the implied value is never multiplied by them. The net (expected) column in the detail view is the only place odds enter the arithmetic.
  • Live-token protocols are set aside. They already trade, so the board shows the market's own value for them below the board, not a fitted one.
